TensorFS keeps derived weights (a LoRA merged into a model's blocks) in the OS's temporary storage, not the store (cl-1318): `/var/tmp/cozy-derived-<uid>/<boot>` on Linux (disk, where `/tmp` is often RAM), the OS's temporary directory elsewhere, never a RAM filesystem or `~/.cozy`. Only this boot's directory is read, and the first process after a reboot removes the rest. A hard cap of an eighth of that filesystem, never into the store's reserve: a page past it displaces pages this process hasn't used, else it is merged again next time. A short store on the same disk gives them back before any model. A page is written whole under a locked temporary name, with no sync; one a killed writer left goes at the next look. The host tier's writer is timed (`persist_ns`), and `Pages.flush` waits for what it was sent.
