TensorD decides no bytes. varena's ledger names who gives way (`Table::victims`: processes in no request, warm-set priority last, then the reload each costs per byte its exit frees, discounted while it sits idle) and TensorD only ends it. A request asks for the room it begins needing on a thread of its own, so idle neighbours give back what they hold, and their contexts go too when that isn't enough, overlapped with the request instead of on its fill path; a foreign program's lasting shortage does the same. A warm `host` level is a priority, not an eviction. No clock ends an executor. The lane is chosen from the card's capacity, never free memory. varena keeps a display GPU's floor itself (TensorFS 0.21.3+; older executors keep TensorD's limit).
